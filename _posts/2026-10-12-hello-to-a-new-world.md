AI. Ugh. At least I now have a reason to blog.

I have a job in software. Who knows for how long?

Ignoring Artificial Intelligence isn't really an option. I need to stay employable. I have a family to provide for.

This is a new "World" to say "Hello" to.

So, this is my story. When, someday in the future, I'm looking for the next thing, I'll document my thoughts, opinions and learning process. Is this better than a resumé? I sure hope so.

"Coding was never the job." I've seen this sentiment *(gestures broadly)* out there. "Building things" and "solving problems" is the job of the Software Engineer. I agree, but how do we do that in a way that is *valued*?

I think the answer is, as it always has been: Expertise.

There's that [proverbial story](https://quoteinvestigator.com/2017/03/06/tap) about the engineer who bills a tiny amount to use the hammer but something absurd for "knowing where to tap it".

The challenge is that AI appears to devalue expertise. Especially in "coding".

So I'm going to be building my expertise a) in using the AI itself, and b) leveraging it to gain more expertise, in more areas, more quickly.
